\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

\begin{spacing}{1.5}

This work presents a pipeline based on locally executed Small Language Models (SLMs) that identifies errors in the logs of the Information and Communication Technology Directorate (DTIC) of the University of Cuenca, notifies them and recommends actions to the developer. The study used the logs of the \textit{ms-users} microservice, whose 223,104 relevant events were grouped into 4,463 batches of 50 events. A batch-level ground truth was built from the vote of three Large Language Models (LLMs) and the review of two DTIC developers, with 130 error batches; although the LLMs almost always agreed with each other (Fleiss' $\kappa$ of 0.886), the developers confirmed only 12.5\% of a sample of batches that all three had marked as errors. Eight SLMs were compared, and Qwen3.5-9B obtained the highest Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC), although, like the other models, it marked as errors many conditions that the institution considers normal. Fine-tuning it with low-rank adapters on the quantized model (QLoRA) reduced false alarms from 113 to 1 on the test split and raised its MCC from 0.41 to 0.95. In a continuous-stream simulation with injected errors, a per-event semantic cache kept the detection of the fine-tuned model while sending only one in 17 batches to the SLM, and the response time was determined by the time needed to fill each batch, which depends on the service traffic, rather than by the pipeline processing. The pipeline includes Slack notifications, a recommender and a dashboard, and all the work was carried out on personal computers with a single graphics card.

\end{spacing}

\hspace{1.27cm}\textit{Author Keywords:} small language models, log analysis, error detection, fine-tuning, semantic cache, observability
